About RestaurantProfitCalc

RestaurantProfitCalc.com  ·  Free tools for hospitality operators

Most restaurant owners can tell you what they took last Saturday night. Far fewer can tell you what they kept. That gap is what this site exists to close.

Why this exists

Restaurant accounting software is either expensive, complicated, or both — and it usually arrives too late. A monthly P&L tells you about a problem four weeks after it started. By then the roster has been written three more times and the supplier invoices are already paid.

What most operators actually need is something much simpler: a way to take last week's revenue, wages, and invoices and see, in under two minutes, whether the business is making money and which cost line is out of shape. That's the whole idea behind the calculator. No signup, no trial, no upsell to a paid tier.

The calculator runs entirely in your browser. Your numbers are never sent to us, stored, or seen by anyone. Close the tab and they're gone.

Where the benchmark numbers come from

Every benchmark on this site — food cost of 28–35%, labour of 28–35%, prime cost under 65%, occupancy under 10% — comes from figures that are widely used across hospitality accounting and industry reporting. They are ranges, not rules, and they're most useful as a first check: if one of your cost lines sits well outside its range, that's where to look first.

Two things worth saying plainly:

  • These ranges vary by market. Wage floors, rent, and supplier costs differ enormously between countries and between a CBD site and a suburban one. Treat the benchmarks as a starting point, not a verdict.
  • They vary by format. A quick-service takeaway and a 40-seat fine dining room have genuinely different cost structures. The ranges on our profit margin guide are broken out by concept for this reason.

If you think one of our figures is wrong or out of date for your market, tell us. We'd rather fix it than defend it.

How the calculator works

Simple mode takes your weekly revenue and four cost percentages and returns your net margin, prime cost, and annualised figures. Detailed mode takes actual dollar amounts across fourteen line items and builds a full weekly P&L, so you can see every cost as a percentage of revenue side by side.

Both modes use the same underlying arithmetic you'd do by hand. There's no proprietary model and nothing hidden — the calculation is revenue minus costs, expressed as a percentage. The value isn't in the maths, it's in having every line laid out in one place with a benchmark next to it.

What this site is not

It's not accounting software, and it's not a substitute for an accountant. It doesn't handle tax, depreciation, loan schedules, or statutory reporting. It won't file anything for you.

The results it produces are indicative. Before you make a decision that matters — signing a lease, taking on debt, restructuring your team — take the numbers to a qualified accountant who knows your business and your jurisdiction.
